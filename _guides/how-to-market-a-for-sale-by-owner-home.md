This guide is for owners selling without an agent who need buyers to find the house, like what they see, and book a showing. You'll get a two-week launch plan, a listing description formula, a photo shot list, and the safety rules that keep strangers in your home from becoming a problem.

## Plan a two-week launch

New listings get the most views in their first days, and buyers who have been searching for months pounce on fresh inventory. Treat launch like a product release: everything finished, then everything at once.

1. **Days −14 to −7:** Finish repairs, declutter, deep clean, order HOA documents, and complete your disclosure forms.
2. **Day −6:** Photograph the home in good weather and daylight, plus a simple floor plan.
3. **Day −5:** Write the description, a one-page feature sheet, and a comparable-sales sheet.
4. **Day −3:** Order the yard sign and open house riders; set your showing windows.
5. **Day 0 (a Thursday):** Go live on listing sites, and in the MLS if you're using a flat-fee broker.
6. **Day 0:** Install the sign and post to neighborhood social groups.
7. **Days 2–3 (the weekend):** Hold open houses both days.
8. **Days 4–10:** Run private showings and ask every visitor for feedback.
9. **Days 10–14:** Review views, showings, and feedback, then adjust photos or price.

## Write a listing description that sells

Buyers skim. Your description should earn the showing in the first two sentences and answer the obvious questions after that. Use this formula:

1. **Headline:** the one thing that sets the home apart.
2. **Opening line:** style, bedrooms, baths, square footage, and the best feature.
3. **Three standouts,** each with a specific: a year, a material, a measurement.
4. **Flow:** a quick walk through the main rooms.
5. **Systems and updates** with years: roof, HVAC, water heater, windows.
6. **Location facts:** distances to parks, transit, schools, and shopping.
7. **Logistics:** showing times, open house dates, and how to submit an offer.

Here's an illustrative example:

> Renovated brick ranch on a quarter-acre with a fenced yard. This three-bedroom, two-bath home has 1,650 square feet on one level, a 2023 kitchen with quartz counters and a gas range, and refinished oak floors throughout. The primary suite adds a walk-in closet and a tiled shower. Roof 2021, furnace and AC 2019, water heater 2024. Half a mile to Riverside Park and the Route 9 bus. Showings by appointment Thursday through Sunday; open house Saturday and Sunday 1–4. Disclosures and a comparable-sales sheet available on request.

Skip filler like "must see" and "won't last." Specifics persuade; adjectives don't.

### Keep your ad within fair housing rules

The Fair Housing Act bars any notice, statement, or advertisement for the sale of a dwelling that indicates a preference, limitation, or discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, disability, familial status, or national origin. That advertising rule applies even to owners selling their own homes who are exempt from other parts of the Act. Many states and cities protect additional classes. The fix is simple: describe the property, not the buyer.

| Avoid | Use instead |
|---|---|
| Perfect for a young family | Three bedrooms, fenced yard, 0.3 miles to Lincoln Elementary |
| Ideal for retirees | Single-level living with a no-step entry |
| Great for singles or couples | Low-maintenance one-bedroom condo with garage parking |
| Safe family neighborhood | Cul-de-sac street with sidewalks |
| Not suitable for wheelchairs | Split-level; all bedrooms are up one flight of stairs |

## Photos that earn the click

Photos decide whether a buyer reads your description at all. Hire a real estate photographer if your budget allows; if not, follow these rules:

- **Shoot in daylight** with every light on and blinds open. Overcast days give soft, even light.
- **Shoot horizontally** from a corner at chest height, so each photo shows three walls.
- **Lead with your best image**, usually the front exterior or the kitchen.
- **Cover everything:** every room, the yard, the street view, the garage, and any feature you mention in the description.
- **Add a floor plan.** Buyers use it to decide whether the layout works before they visit.
- **Don't edit away reality.** Brightening is fine; hiding a cracked driveway just wastes everyone's time.

A short, steady walkthrough video helps out-of-town buyers and works well on social media. Record it in one continuous pass from the front door, moving slowly, with the same lights-on, blinds-open setup as your photos.

## Yard sign, flyers, and neighbors

Your yard sign works around the clock. Keep it readable from a moving car: For Sale By Owner, a way to reach you, and a short web address or QR code that opens the listing. Add an "Open Sat–Sun 1–4" rider on weekends. Check HOA rules and local sign ordinances first.

Print a one-page **feature sheet** with the best photo, key facts, updates with years, average monthly utility costs, property taxes, and HOA dues. Next to it, put a **comparable-sales sheet** showing recent nearby sales that support your price. Buyers' agents notice a seller who prices from data.

Then tell your neighbors. They know who wants to move closer to family, and they're often your best word-of-mouth marketers.

## Listing sites, the MLS, and social media

- **By-owner marketplaces:** list with full photos, the floor plan, and your description, and reply to inquiries within a couple of hours.
- **The MLS:** a flat-fee broker can put your home in front of agents and the many public search sites that pull MLS data. See [flat-fee MLS explained](/guides/flat-fee-mls-explained/) to weigh the cost.
- **Social media:** post the listing link and a walkthrough video to neighborhood groups and your own profile, and ask friends to share. Never post when you'll be away from home or details about your schedule.

### Respond fast and track every lead

Buyers who contact a by-owner seller are usually comparing several homes, so speed wins. Reply within a couple of hours and offer two specific showing times instead of asking when they're free. Log every inquiry in a simple spreadsheet: name, how they found you, pre-approval status, showing date, and feedback. After the first week, that log shows which channel actually produces showings, so you can spend your time there. Follow up once with everyone who toured; a short note asking whether they have questions often surfaces an offer, or an objection you can fix.

## Open houses and private showings

Open houses work best in the first weekend. Set out feature sheets, the comp sheet, and a sign-in sheet, and keep disclosures available for serious buyers. Have a second adult with you so one of you can greet visitors while the other moves through the house.

### Showing safety checklist

- Show by appointment only, and confirm full name and phone number in advance.
- For private showings, ask for a lender pre-approval letter or proof of funds.
- Check a photo ID at the door.
- Keep a second adult home, and tell a friend your schedule.
- Lock away valuables, medications, mail, spare keys, and documents.
- Walk behind visitors, keep your phone on you, and stay near an exit.
- Schedule showings in daylight when possible.
- If you have security cameras, disclose them. Some states require consent from everyone recorded for audio, so check before recording conversations.

## Spot scams aimed at sellers

Real estate fraud is big business. The FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center counted 12,368 real estate fraud complaints and $275.1 million in reported losses in 2025, a category that includes closing-related wire fraud as well as rental and other property scams. Watch for these patterns:

- **Overpayment checks.** A buyer sends a cashier's check for more than the deposit and asks you to wire back the difference. The check bounces after your wire is gone.
- **Sight-unseen pressure.** A buyer who is "overseas," won't visit, and insists on their own escrow agent or attorney.
- **Fake wiring instructions.** Emails that appear to come from your title company with "updated" payment details. The CFPB advises confirming any instructions by calling a number you already know, never one in the email.
- **Hijacked listings.** Scammers copy your photos into fake rental ads to collect deposits. Search your address online every few days while you're listed.
