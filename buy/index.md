## Why buy a home directly from the owner?

When you buy from an owner, you negotiate with the decision-maker. That can mean faster answers, more flexible terms — a rent-back, a quicker close, included furniture, or even [seller financing](/guides/owner-financing-explained/) — and a seller who isn't paying a listing commission and may have more room on price.

## How to buy by owner safely

1. **Get pre-approved first.** A lender's pre-approval letter tells the owner you're serious and shows you your real budget. Check your range with the [affordability calculator](/tools/home-affordability-calculator/).
2. **Research the price.** Pull recent nearby sales and compare price per square foot. Owners sometimes overprice — evidence keeps the conversation calm.
3. **Put it in writing.** Use a state-approved purchase contract or have a real estate attorney draft one with inspection, appraisal and financing contingencies.
4. **Use a neutral closing agent.** A title company or attorney holds earnest money, checks the title and handles the deed — never wire money to an individual.
5. **Verify everything.** Confirm ownership in county records and verify wiring instructions by phone using a number you find independently. Our [scam guide](/guides/avoid-scams-buying-selling-renting-by-owner/) lists the red flags.

## Do I need a buyer's agent?

You can hire one, use an attorney only, or go it alone. Since August 2024, buyers sign a written agreement with their agent before touring, and the agent's pay is negotiated up front — sellers may or may not contribute. Read [how commission rules changed](/guides/real-estate-commission-rule-changes-explained/) before you decide.
