## Selling your business by owner

Most small businesses are valued on cash flow. For owner-operated companies, buyers usually start with **seller's discretionary earnings (SDE)** — profit plus the owner's salary and one-time or personal expenses — and apply a multiple that reflects risk, growth and how dependent the business is on you.

| Step | What to prepare |
|---|---|
| Valuation | Three years of P&Ls, tax returns, SDE add-backs |
| Teaser listing | Industry, region, revenue range — no name |
| Buyer qualification | NDA, proof of funds or lender pre-qualification |
| Letter of intent | Price, structure, timeline, exclusivity |
| Due diligence | Financials, contracts, leases, licenses, staff |
| Closing | Purchase agreement, transition plan, any seller note |

Many deals combine a bank loan (often an SBA 7(a) loan) with a seller note. Read the full [guide to selling a business by owner](/guides/how-to-sell-a-business-by-owner/) and bring in a CPA and a business attorney for structure and taxes.

## Confidential by default

Your ByOwners listing can stay anonymous — industry, region and financial ranges only — until you approve a buyer and they sign an NDA. Inquiries come to us first, so your staff and customers don't find out from a public ad.
