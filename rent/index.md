## For landlords: rent out your property by owner

Self-managing a rental can save the management fee and keeps you in control of who lives in your property. Do it right and you'll have fewer vacancies and fewer surprises:

1. **Run the numbers** with the [rental property calculator](/tools/rental-property-calculator/) — include vacancy, maintenance and capital reserves.
2. **Price it from comps.** Our [rent pricing guide](/guides/how-to-set-the-right-rent-price/) shows how a $50 overprice can cost you a month of rent.
3. **Write fair, consistent screening criteria** and apply them to everyone — see the [tenant screening guide](/guides/tenant-screening-guide-for-landlords/) and [fair housing rules](/guides/fair-housing-rules-for-private-landlords/).
4. **Use a solid lease** with your state's required disclosures — start with [lease agreement essentials](/guides/lease-agreement-essentials/).
5. **[List your rental free](/sell/?cat=rental)** and let inquiries come to you privately.

## For renters: rent directly from the owner

Renting from an owner can mean faster decisions, no broker fee and a landlord who knows the property well. Protect yourself:

- **Tour in person** (or by live video with someone you trust) before paying anything.
- **Verify ownership** through the county property records and make sure the person showing the unit is the owner or authorized manager.
- **Never pay with wire transfers, gift cards or crypto.** Rental scams often copy real listings and ask for a deposit to "hold" the unit. Our [scam guide](/guides/avoid-scams-buying-selling-renting-by-owner/) shows the patterns.
- **Get the lease in writing** before handing over any money.
