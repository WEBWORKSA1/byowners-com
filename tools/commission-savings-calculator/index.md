## How this calculator works

The math is deliberately transparent:

- **Traditional cost** = sale price × the total commission percentage you entered.
- **By-owner cost** = sale price × any buyer-agent fee you choose to offer, plus your out-of-pocket costs.
- **Savings** = traditional cost − by-owner cost.

Example: on a $400,000 sale, a 5.5% total commission is $22,000. If you sell by owner, offer a 2.5% buyer-agent fee ($10,000) and spend $1,500 on a flat-fee listing, photos and an attorney, your cost is $11,500 — you keep **$10,500** more. If the buyer is unrepresented and you offer no fee, you keep about $20,500.

## Three ways to use the result

1. **Decide whether by owner is worth it.** If the savings are meaningful to you, read the [complete guide to selling by owner](/guides/how-to-sell-your-house-by-owner/) and take the [readiness quiz](/tools/fsbo-readiness-quiz/).
2. **Negotiate smarter.** Commissions have always been negotiable. Knowing the dollar value of each percentage point helps whether you list with an agent or not — see [how commission rules changed](/guides/real-estate-commission-rule-changes-explained/).
3. **Plan your net.** Plug the by-owner cost into the [seller net proceeds calculator](/tools/seller-net-proceeds-calculator/) to see your cash at closing.

## What the calculator does not include

Savings assume the same sale price either way. Agents argue they achieve higher prices; owners counter that pricing to the comps produces the same result. The honest answer: price drives everything, so study the comps carefully with our [pricing guide](/guides/how-to-price-a-home-for-sale-by-owner/) before you list.
