## How your payment is built

A mortgage payment usually has five parts, often called **PITI + extras**:

1. **Principal** — pays down the loan balance.
2. **Interest** — the lender's charge, front-loaded in early years.
3. **Taxes** — property taxes, often collected monthly into escrow.
4. **Insurance** — homeowners insurance, also usually escrowed.
5. **HOA and PMI** — association dues and private mortgage insurance if you put down less than 20% on a conventional loan.

The principal-and-interest formula is the standard amortization formula: payment = loan × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n), where r is the monthly rate and n the number of payments.

## Reading the amortization chart

The red area is your loan balance; the green area is your equity (home price minus balance, before appreciation). In the early years, most of each payment is interest, which is why extra principal payments early on save the most.
